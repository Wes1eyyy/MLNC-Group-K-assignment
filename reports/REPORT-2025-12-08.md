## Executive Summary

This report presents an **enhanced evaluation** of 8 machine learning algorithms for predicting English Premier League match outcomes, building upon our previous work from November 24, 2025. The models were trained on the same 8,640 historical matches but with **enriched features** including team ages, market values, and previous season rankings.

**Key Findings:**
- **Best Performing Model:** Random Forest (52.40% accuracy, +0.63% improvement)
- **Most Improved Model:** Multi-Layer Perceptron (51.77% accuracy, +6.35% improvement)
- **Fastest Training:** Gaussian Naive Bayes (<1 second)
- **New Features:** +6 features (team age, market value, previous season rank)

**Performance Summary vs. Previous Version (Nov 24):**
- 5 models improved (Random Forest, MLP, SVM, KNN, Decision Tree)
- 3 models slightly decreased (Naive Bayes, XGBoost, Logistic Regression)
- Average improvement: +1.12% across all models

## 2. Dataset Overview

### 2.1 Data Split
- **Total Matches:** 9,600
- **Training Set:** 8,640 matches (90%) | Date Range: 19/08/2000 - 03/04/2023
- **Testing Set:** 960 matches (10%) | Date Range: 04/04/2023 - 25/05/2025
- **Features:** 26 engineered features (20 original + 6 new)

### 2.2 Class Distribution
The dataset exhibits class imbalance:
- **Home Win (H):** 45.9% (3,969 matches)
- **Away Win (A):** 29.1% (2,518 matches)
- **Draw (D):** 24.9% (2,153 matches)

This reflects the well-known home advantage in football.

---

## 3. Feature Engineering

### 3.1 Feature Categories

#### 3.1.1 Original Features (20)
All features are rolling averages computed from historical match data:

1. **Offensive Statistics:**
   - Average Goals Scored (Home/Away)
   - Average Shots (Home/Away)
   - Average Shots on Target (Home/Away)
   - Average Corners (Home/Away)

2. **Defensive Statistics:**
   - Average Goals Conceded (Home/Away)
   - Average Shots Conceded (Home/Away)
   - Average Corners Conceded (Home/Away)

3. **Form Indicators:**
   - Recent Wins
   - Recent Losses
   - Recent Draws

#### 3.1.2 New Features (6)

4. **Squad Quality Indicators:**
   - **Average Age:** Indicates squad maturity and experience
     - Young squads (<24 years): More energetic, less experienced
     - Prime age squads (24-28): Optimal balance
     - Veteran squads (>28): More experienced, potentially slower

   - **Average Market Value:** Proxy for squad strength and quality
     - Correlates with team investment and expected performance
     - Reflects individual player quality

   - **Previous Season Rank:** Historical performance indicator
     - Strong predictor of current season form
     - Accounts for team trajectory (improving/declining)

### 3.2 Temporal Splitting Strategy
To prevent data leakage, we employed **temporal train-test split** instead of random splitting. This ensures the model is tested on future matches it has never seen.

## 7. Challenges and Limitations

### 7.1 Inherent Unpredictability of Football
- Football matches are influenced by many factors not in our dataset:
  - Player injuries and suspensions
  - Weather conditions
  - Referee decisions
  - Team motivation and psychology
  - Tactical changes during the match

### 7.2 Class Imbalance
- Draws (24.9%) are significantly underrepresented
- Models tend to favor the majority class (Home Wins)
- Class weighting helped but didn't fully solve the issue

### 7.3 Feature Limitations
- Squad-level aggregates (average age, average value)
  - Don't capture individual star players
  - Miss squad depth and injury situations
- No player-level information
- No venue-specific factors (pitch dimensions, climate)
- No tactical information (formation, playing style)

### 7.4 Temporal Factors
- Team composition changes over seasons
- League competitiveness evolves
- Tactical trends shift over time
- Market value inflation over 25 years not adjusted

### 7.5 Data Quality Issues
- Missing data for newly promoted teams
- Inconsistent team naming across data sources
- Market values may not reflect current form

## 8. Conclusions and Recommendations

### 8.1 Key Findings

1. **Random Forest Remains the Best Model (52.40% accuracy)**
   - Improved by 0.63% with new features
   - Balances accuracy, speed, and robustness
   - Provides interpretable feature importance
   - Recommended for deployment

2. **MLP Shows Dramatic Improvement (+6.35%)**
   - Now competitive with tree-based models (51.77%)
   - Demonstrates value of deep learning with sufficient features
   - Requires more training time but viable for production

3. **New Features Provide Meaningful Value**
   - Average improvement: +1.12% across all models
   - Squad quality (age, value, rank) complements match statistics
   - Away team features more predictive than home team's

4. **Draw Prediction Still the Biggest Challenge**
   - All models struggle with Draw class
   - Best draw recall: 36% (Logistic Regression)
   - May require specialized approaches

### 8.2 Recommendations for Future Improvement

#### 8.2.1 Data Enhancement
- **Player-level statistics:**
  - Top scorer, assists, key player injuries
  - Squad depth and rotation patterns

- **Tactical information:**
  - Formation (4-4-2, 4-3-3, etc.)
  - Playing style (possession, counter-attack)
  - Manager effect and tenure

- **Contextual data:**
  - Weather conditions (temperature, precipitation)
  - Stadium characteristics
  - Derby matches and rivalry intensity
  - Match importance (title race, relegation battle)

- **Head-to-head records:**
  - Historical matchups between specific teams
  - Recent form in direct encounters

#### 8.2.2 Feature Engineering
- **Interaction features:**
  - HomeValue × HomeAge (squad balance)
  - AwayShots × HomeDefense (offensive-defensive matchup)
  - PrevRank × RecentForm (trajectory indicator)

- **Ratio features:**
  - HomeValue / AwayValue (squad quality gap)
  - HomeAge - AwayAge (experience differential)

- **Time-series features:**
  - Form trajectory (improving/declining)
  - Momentum indicators
  - Exponentially weighted moving averages

- **Market value normalization:**
  - Adjust for inflation over 25 years
  - Normalize by league average per season

#### 8.2.3 Model Improvements
- **Ensemble Methods:**
  - Combine Random Forest + MLP + XGBoost
  - Weighted voting by validation performance
  - Stacking with meta-learner

- **Specialized Classifiers:**
  - Separate binary classifier for draws
  - Hierarchical model: (Win vs. Not Win) → (Home vs. Away)
  - Ordinal regression: model outcomes as ordered categories

- **Deep Learning Architectures:**
  - Wider MLP (more neurons per layer)
  - Residual connections to prevent vanishing gradients
  - Attention mechanisms for feature importance

- **Hyperparameter Optimization:**
  - Grid search for all models
  - Bayesian optimization for MLP
  - Cross-validation for robust estimates

#### 8.2.4 Evaluation Strategies
- **Better metrics:**
  - F1-score weighted by class importance
  - AUC-ROC for probabilistic predictions
  - Calibration curves for probability accuracy

- **Business-oriented evaluation:**
  - Betting ROI simulation
  - Risk-adjusted predictions
  - Confidence intervals

- **Temporal validation:**
  - Rolling window cross-validation
  - Test on multiple future seasons
  - Stability analysis over time

### 8.3 Final Remarks

This enhanced study demonstrates several important findings:

1. **Feature engineering significantly impacts model performance**
   - MLP improved by 6.35% with just 6 additional features
   - Shows importance of domain knowledge in feature selection

2. **Different models benefit differently from new features**
   - Deep learning (MLP): Large improvement
   - Tree-based (Random Forest, Decision Tree): Moderate improvement
   - Linear (Logistic Regression): Minimal impact
   - Probabilistic (Naive Bayes): Slight decrease (independence assumption)

3. **52.40% accuracy represents meaningful predictive power**
   - Significantly better than random guessing (33.3%)
   - Better than baseline (45.9% by always predicting Home Win)
   - But still limited by inherent unpredictability of football

4. **Ensemble methods consistently outperform single models**
   - Random Forest (ensemble) > Decision Tree (single)
   - Suggests future work should focus on combining multiple models

5. **The "Draw problem" requires specialized attention**
   - Traditional features don't distinguish draws well
   - May need features specific to draw scenarios
   - Defensive tactics, cautious play, evenly matched teams

**Practical Implications:**
- For **deployment**: Use Random Forest (52.40%, fast, robust)
- For **research**: Explore MLP architectures (51.77%, high potential)
- For **betting**: Need better draw prediction before practical use
- For **insights**: Feature importance shows shots and goals matter most

The journey from 51.77% (Nov 24) to 52.40% (Dec 8) demonstrates that incremental improvements are possible through thoughtful feature engineering. However, the inherent unpredictability of football remains a fundamental challenge that no amount of data alone can fully overcome.
